# What public disclosures say about e-money token reserves under MiCA

Generated by `scripts/report.py` from `outputs/`. Method and limits: `METHOD.md`.

## 1. Summary

- USDC's published reserve on 31 August 2026 held 13.1% in bank deposits and 84.9% in units of one US government money market fund, the Circle Reserve Fund. MiCA's rules bind the reserve of Circle's EU issuer, which is not published separately: the figures describe the global reserve, not the EU one.
- Read through the fund, 82.4% of that reserve is available within one working day, far above the 40% of the draft rules for a significant token. Without the fund, whose units the EBA's draft on eligible instruments does not admit, 13.2% is.
- The largest one-day net outflow of the reference episodes (TUSD, January 2024, 13.3% of supply) is covered 6.2 times by the one-day assets read through the fund, and 0.99 times without the fund. USDC's own worst day since it reached its present size, 3.6% on 2023-03-12, is covered 3.6 times even without the fund.
- EURC, EURCV and USDCV are held in bank deposits and meet every rule on deposits and liquidity buckets. SG-FORGE names its banks: EURCV is 100.0% at one bank and USDCV 77.6%, above the 25% per-bank cap of the draft rules, which bind a non-significant token only if its supervisor requires them.
- Circle does not name its banks and nobody publishes deposit notice periods, so bank concentration, the risk behind USDC's loss of its peg in March 2023, cannot be checked from public data for USDC or EURC.

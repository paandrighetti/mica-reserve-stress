# What public disclosures say about e-money token reserves under MiCA

Generated by `scripts/report.py` from `outputs/`. MiCA is the EU's Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation. Method, sources and limits: `METHOD.md`.

## 1. Summary

- On 31 August 2026, USDC's published reserve held 13.1% in bank deposits and 84.9% in units of one US government money market fund, the Circle Reserve Fund. MiCA's rules bind the reserve of Circle's EU issuer, which is not published separately, so these figures describe the global reserve. Its deposits would sit below the 30% that MiCA requires of a token that is not significant, and below the 60% that the draft standards set for a significant one, which USDC becomes on 12 November 2026.
- Counting the fund's own holdings, 82.4% of the reserve is available within one working day, against 40% in the draft standards for a significant token. On our reading of the European Banking Authority's (EBA) draft on eligible instruments, units of a US fund do not count; without them, assets available within a day fall to 13.2%.
- The largest one-day net outflow among the reference episodes was 7.3% of supply (BUSD, stop of minting, February to April 2023). USDC's assets available within a day amount to 11.3 times that outflow with the fund and 1.8 times without it. USDC's own worst day at a comparable size (supply at least a tenth of today's) was 3.6%, on 12 March 2023.
- EURC, EURCV and USDCV hold their reserves in bank deposits, so they meet the deposit floors and the liquidity buckets, deposits being treated as available within a working day. Societe Generale-FORGE names its banks: EURCV is 100.0% at one bank and USDCV 77.6%, above any per-bank cap of the draft standards (25% for a systemically important bank, 15% or 5% otherwise). Those caps bind a token that is not significant only if its supervisor requires them.
- Circle's examination reports do not name its banks. Bank concentration is what broke USDC's peg in March 2023, when 3.3 billion dollars of its reserve were held at Silicon Valley Bank (Circle, 11 March 2023, reported by Reuters). Its weekly transparency page reportedly splits deposits between systemically important and other banks, which this study could not capture.
